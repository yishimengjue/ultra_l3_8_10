Overall goal:
- Sound like web-page QA, not a textbook exercise set, reading-comprehension analysis, or outline summary.
- Questions are short, specific, and SELF-CONTAINED; answers are faithful, concise, and traceable sentence-by-sentence to the document.
- Direct factual questions are the backbone, but do not degrade into a flat "what is / list the" extraction dump. When the evidence is clear, naturally include true/false, multiple-choice, why (causal), or compare questions.
- Safety is not asking less: if a complex question is unstable, prefer turning it into an answerable factual question or a conservative true/false on the same information point, and keep the count near the requested range.
- Do NOT ask meta-questions about the text itself ("what type of question is this", "is this a true/false question", "what genre is this passage").

First select information anchors internally; do not output the process:
- Prioritize the most salient core facts, entities (people/organizations/places), dates, numbers/parameters/steps, conditions/causes/contrasts, exceptions/limits, and opening/closing conclusions.
- Each QA asks about ONE clear focus; do not fuse several unrelated numbers or adjacent selling points into one answer.
- If a title or heading names "A vs B / the difference between A and B" but the body only explains A: ask about A's concrete information; if needed, ask whether the text states anything about B and answer "not stated / cannot be determined" — never fill in B from outside knowledge.
- If the text has explicit limiting, exception, or hedging words (only, except, possibly, about, estimated, up to), preserve them in both question and answer.

Soft question-type strategy (NO fixed quotas — let the evidence decide):
1. Direct factual questions dominate. Ask about entities, dates, places, quantities, names, functions, steps, causes, results. Make the question naturally specific without stuffing a whole paragraph into it.
2. Let true/false appear naturally when the text states a clear truth/falsity, capability, suitability, or listed/not-listed relation. Judge a real document fact or an information boundary ("not stated"), never a question type or reading behavior. Answer with True/False/Cannot be determined first, then one short clause of textual evidence.
3. Use why/cause questions cautiously but do not avoid them entirely. Only when the text explicitly gives a relation ("because, since, in order to, so that, leads to, causes, results in, due to"). Give the conclusion first, then the shortest causal evidence; add no common-sense or motive not in the text.
4. Make multiple-choice verifiable. The stem MUST contain four options A/B/C/D (a "which is NOT" negative form is welcome when supported). Single-answer only: exactly one option is correct; distractors stay within the same context and introduce no outside knowledge. Answer with the correct option's content first, then one short clause of evidence.
5. Use compare/condition questions sparingly, only when the text explicitly supplies both objects or the condition. If one side is missing, ask about the given side or answer "the text does not provide information on the other object". One-hop reasoning only; no multi-step calculation or invented numbers.

Language-neutral core constraints (always hold):
1. Use only information explicit in the document. If something is not stated, answer "not stated / cannot be determined"; do not extrapolate, correct, or elevate the text.
2. Ignore navigation, ads, footers, cookie/login prompts, share/subscribe tails, and contact/boilerplate; build questions only from the substantive body.
3. For structured source text (tables of contents, module/qualification lists, step lists, product spec lists, problem sets): ask only about its EXPLICIT structure (which item, which step, what is listed, what a task requires) — do not solve it or infer the principle behind a listed heading.
4. The subject of each answer must be locatable in the same or an adjacent clause; pronouns ("this/that/it/the former/the latter/the above") must have a clear referent. Keep boundary words (only/except/possibly) in the QA.

English-specific source constraints (grounded in the observed EN L2 noise):
- Q&A-site / forum chrome: skip "Lost your password", "Please briefly explain why you feel this question/answer should be reported", "Get access to", account-creation and reporting prompts; ask only about the substantive answer body.

- "Next Read" / "Related" / "Click here" link tails are noise; ignore them.
- Cracked-software / SEO-spam pages with broken grammar: answer only what is literally stated; do not repair the grammar into new claims or endorse the product.
- Embedded non-English fragments (e.g. Russian/Polish blocks): ask only about the English portion; do not translate or answer from the non-English text.
- OCR / broken math or symbols (e.g. "Φ2}2", "xy=xy", split decimals): quote the fragment literally; do not "fix" numbers, expand symbols, or invent an equation the text does not state.

- Do NOT open answers with "according to the text / in this article / as mentioned above / the document states" — questions and answers must be self-contained.
- Add no background, common-sense inference, evaluation, motive, or conclusion absent from the text.
